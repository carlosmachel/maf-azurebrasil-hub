Project Atlas - Internal Planning Notes

Overview

Project Atlas is the codename for the new customer analytics dashboard. The project lead is Bruno
Alves, working alongside a small team of frontend engineers, a data scientist, and a product designer.
The approved budget for Project Atlas is $180,000, covering design, frontend engineering, and a
third-party data visualization license. This document captures the product scope, the data pipeline
design, the rollout plan, the risk register, and the metrics we will use to judge whether Atlas is
actually moving the needle for account managers once it ships.

Background

Account managers currently piece together churn signals from four different internal tools and a
handful of manually maintained spreadsheets. That process is slow, error prone, and means churn signals
often surface days after they would have been actionable. Atlas exists to collapse that workflow into a
single dashboard that surfaces the same signals automatically, with enough lead time for an account
manager to actually intervene before a customer disengages entirely.

Product Scope

The dashboard will surface churn predictions and usage trends to account managers. In scope for the
initial release are: a churn risk score per account updated daily, a usage trend chart covering the
trailing ninety days, and an alerting rule that flags any account whose churn score crosses a
configurable threshold. Out of scope for this release are predictive lifetime value modeling and
automated outreach triggers, both of which are being considered for a follow-up project once Atlas has
been validated in production. The target launch date is October 1st.

Data Pipeline

Usage events are ingested from the product telemetry stream and aggregated nightly into a feature store
that feeds the churn prediction model. The model itself is a gradient-boosted classifier retrained
weekly on a rolling twelve-month window of labeled churn outcomes. Feature freshness is monitored
separately from model accuracy, since a stale feature pipeline can silently degrade predictions without
the model itself ever being retrained on bad data. Data visualization in the dashboard is rendered
client-side using the licensed third-party charting library, chosen specifically because it handles
large time-series datasets without the performance issues we hit with our previous in-house charting
components.

Rollout Plan

The rollout will start with a beta group of 12 enterprise accounts before going to general availability.
Beta accounts were selected to cover a mix of account sizes, industries, and existing churn risk levels,
so that feedback reflects more than just our friendliest customers. Beta feedback is collected through a
combination of in-app surveys and a biweekly call with each participating account manager. General
availability is gated on beta accounts reporting that the churn score meaningfully matches their own
qualitative read of the account at least 80% of the time.

Key Risks

Key risks: the data visualization vendor's contract renewal is still pending legal review, and a delay
there could push the licensing terms past the planned launch date. The churn prediction model's accuracy
has not yet been validated against last quarter's actual outcomes, since the labeled data for that
quarter only recently became available, and there is a chance the validation surfaces a meaningful
accuracy gap that requires a model revision before general availability. There is also a staffing risk:
the data scientist on the project is splitting time with another initiative, which has already pushed
the model retraining work out by a week once this quarter.

Testing Strategy

Testing for Atlas includes a backtest of the churn model against the last four quarters of historical
outcomes, a load test of the dashboard against a simulated account base ten times larger than our
current customer count, and a manual accessibility review of the dashboard ahead of the beta launch.
Any backtest accuracy below the agreed 80% threshold blocks the beta launch until the model team
signs off on a remediation plan.

Stakeholders and Communication

Stakeholders include the account management leadership team, the data science team, and the customer
success operations group, who will own the alerting workflow once an account is flagged. Status updates
go out every other Friday in the #project-atlas channel, summarizing beta feedback, model performance,
and any changes to the general availability timeline. Bruno Alves reviews beta feedback weekly with the
account management leads and adjusts the dashboard's alert thresholds based on what the beta group
reports as noisy or actionable.

Success Criteria

Success criteria for Atlas are defined as: the model's churn predictions aligning with account manager
judgment at least 80% of the time during beta, a measurable reduction in the time it takes an account
manager to identify an at-risk account, and a smooth transition from the 12-account beta to general
availability without a feature freeze longer than one week. A secondary success criterion is account
manager adoption, since a dashboard nobody opens isn't actually solving the original problem.

Open Questions

Open questions still being tracked include whether the alerting threshold should be configurable per
account manager rather than fixed globally, and whether the predictive lifetime value modeling work
should be pulled into this project's next phase given how much of the underlying feature pipeline is
already shared. Both questions are expected to be resolved once the beta feedback has been fully
reviewed.

Budget Breakdown

Of the $180,000 approved budget, roughly $90,000 covers the frontend engineering work, including the
churn risk score views and the alerting configuration screen, $50,000 covers the third-party data
visualization license for the first year, and the remaining $40,000 is allocated to the data scientist's
time for model development and the weekly retraining pipeline. Bruno Alves tracks spend against this
breakdown monthly and flags any overage to the account management leadership team before it becomes a
blocker for the general availability launch.
